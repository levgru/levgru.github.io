Hi!

I’m Lev, a physics Ph.D. student at Boston College in Professor Benedetta Flebus’s group, working at the intersection of quantum sensing and quantum many-body theory. I graduated from Harvey Mudd College in 2026 with a B.S. in Joint Mathematics and Physics (High Distinction, Honors in Physics), where I researched adaptive entanglement witnessing with Professor Theresa Lynn, and I have interned at MITRE in quantum theory and integrated photonics.

Outside of physics, I directed HMC’s New Student Orientation, founded the HMC Simulation Racing Club, and played outside hitter for the Claremont men’s club volleyball team. Feel free to reach out by email or through the Contact page.

Earlier Projects

  • Generating Biased Random Entanglement Witnesses May 2025
    Research proposal on using random matrix theory and representation theory to generate entanglement witnesses biased by partial information about an unknown state.
  • Simulating Long-Term Stair Wear (COMAP MCM 2025) January 2025
    Physical and chemical model of stone stair wear for archaeologists, using a 2D Generalized Maxwell Model and Gaussian Mixture Model–based erosion. Paper · Code
  • Modeling Bio-Materials to Second Order Fall 2024
    Chaotic dynamics of masses joined by non-linear springs, a simple model of polymeric biological materials. Write-up · Code
  • Ammonia Umbrella Inversion via Variational Quantum Eigensolvers Spring 2024
    Calculated the symmetric double-well potential of NH3 with Qiskit VQEs on simulated and real (IBM Kyoto) quantum computers. Write-up · Code
